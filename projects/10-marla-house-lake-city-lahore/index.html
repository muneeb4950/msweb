Project Brief

This case study covers a full house construction project we completed for a client in Lake City, Lahore: a 10 Marla plot, built from grey structure through complete finishing under one fixed, itemized quote. It's a fair example of what building a full-sized family home with us actually looks like, stage by stage, not just the sales pitch.

Lake City is where more of our completed houses sit than any other single society in Lahore, so this project also reflects a pattern we see repeatedly there: consistent soil and drainage conditions across the society, which means getting the grey structure specification right the first time pays off later at the finishing stage, something this project bore out directly, according to the client's own review further down this page.

Location & Plot Details

The project sits in Lake City, Lahore, on a 10 Marla plot, roughly 3,025 square feet of land. Lake City is a large, established gated community on Raiwind Road, with its own internal road network, utilities, and society-level oversight of new construction.

Like most Lake City plots, this one came with the consistent soil conditions typical of the society, which simplified foundation planning. No unusual excavation or non-standard foundation work was needed here, keeping the project on our standard construction timeline rather than requiring the extended foundation work that difficult soil sometimes demands elsewhere in Lahore.

Scope of Work

This was a full house construction project, meaning our scope covered every phase from the first excavation to a move-in-ready home: grey structure, finishing, and furnishing, all under a single fixed quote rather than the client coordinating separate contractors for each stage.

Grey structure covered excavation, foundation and DPC, RCC columns, beams and floor slabs, brickwork, and the roof structure. Finishing covered internal and external plaster, paint, electrical wiring and sanitary fittings, tiling, and doors and windows. Furnishing covered kitchen cabinets, wardrobes, and interior lighting, the details that turn a finished structure into a livable home. The client managed one point of contact and one quote for the entire process.

Site Considerations in Lake City

Building inside a large gated community like Lake City comes with a few considerations that don't apply on an open plot. Material deliveries and site access have to work around the society's own gate timings, and any structural change mid-project needs to stay within what's actually buildable on a 10 Marla Lake City plot without triggering additional society-level review.

We plan around these upfront rather than discovering them mid-project: confirming gate access hours, coordinating material delivery schedules, and finalising the design before breaking ground so that walls, staircases, and room layouts don't need to move once construction starts. Changes like that are one of the most common causes of cost and schedule overruns on any construction project, not just in Lake City.

How We Structured Payment for This Project

Like every fixed-quote project, payment was tied to milestones rather than collected upfront as a lump sum: an initial payment to begin site mobilisation and foundation work, further payments released as grey structure stages (columns and beams, roof slab, brickwork) were completed and verified, and a final payment structure covering finishing and furnishing once those stages were underway.

Tying payment to visible, verifiable progress protects the client, not just us: no stage gets paid for until it's actually done to specification, and the client always knows what they've paid for and what's left. The full milestone schedule is part of the written, itemized quote issued at the free site visit, so there are no surprises partway through.

Quality Checks We Followed

As with every project, quality checks ran through each stage rather than waiting for a single final inspection. Before excavation, we confirmed the site layout against the approved drawings. During foundation work, we verified depth, reinforcement, and concrete quality before allowing the pour to proceed.

Brick masonry was checked for line, level, and correct mortar ratio as work progressed, and steel reinforcement, bar spacing, and slab thickness were confirmed before each concrete pour. Concrete curing was never skipped or shortened, since it's what gives the structure its intended strength, and washroom and roof areas were waterproofed as a planned step, not an afterthought once problems appeared.
