Understanding Grey Structure Costs in Bahria Town

The project above gives one real example, but the question we hear most from Bahria Town clients is simpler: what does a grey structure actually cost? Since Bahria Town has its own construction bylaws and inspection process, costs here tend to run a little differently than in other parts of Lahore, so we've laid out the numbers and the reasoning below.

"Grey structure" is the core framework of a house, everything needed to make it structurally complete, before finishing work begins. In Bahria Town this includes excavation, foundation work, brick masonry, columns and beams, roof slabs, staircases, plastering, and basic concealed plumbing and electrical conduits, built to match your approved architectural and structural drawings and the society's own standards.

Covered Area for a 5 Marla House in Bahria Town

A standard 5 Marla plot in Bahria Town measures roughly 25 ft × 45 ft, about 1,125 square feet of plot area. Most homeowners build double-story, and depending on the design and how much open space is left, the covered area usually comes out to around 1,900 to 2,200 square feet, since material and labor are priced per square foot of covered area, this range is what drives the total cost below.

Grey Structure Rate & Total Cost

Our current 5 Marla grey structure rate is PKR 2,950 per square foot (A+ category, slab measurement), the same rate published on our Projects page. For a covered area of 1,900 to 2,200 square feet, that works out to an estimated PKR 5.6 million to PKR 6.5 million for the grey structure.

This is an estimate, not a quote, the exact figure depends on your working and structural drawings, so we confirm the final number after a free site visit. A lower quoted rate elsewhere can look attractive, but it often means a compromise somewhere: thinner steel, a rushed curing process, or less site supervision, all of which show up as cracks or seepage later.

What the Grey Structure Cost Includes

Excavation and foundation work suited to the site's soil conditions, RCC footings, columns and beams, brick masonry at the correct mortar ratio, roof slab casting, staircases, parapet walls, and the rooftop mumty (stair tower) are all part of the grey structure.

It also covers internal and external plastering, concealed electrical piping, plumbing lines, proper concrete curing, waterproofing in wet areas, and basic site safety measures, the same specifications we use across every grey structure project, in Bahria Town or elsewhere.

Why Grey Structure Costs More in Bahria Town

Bahria Town enforces its own construction bylaws and inspections. Material quality, the front elevation design, and structural safety are all checked, and any deviation from the approved drawings can mean fines or rework, both of which add cost.

Approved architectural and structural drawings are required before construction starts, and those approval fees feed into the overall project cost. Bahria Town's expected build quality is also simply higher, which means skilled labor and better materials, not shortcuts.

Good To Know

Factors Affecting Grey Structure Costs

1

Material Prices

Cement, steel reinforcement, bricks, sand, and aggregate prices shift over time, and they're the single biggest driver of grey structure cost.

2

Design Complexity

Large spans, cantilevered sections, basements, and unusual architectural features all need extra steel and concrete.

3

Labor Expertise

Skilled masons, steel fixers, and shuttering carpenters cost more, but they're what keeps the structure sound.

4

Soil & Site Access

Difficult soil or a hard-to-access plot can mean deeper foundations or higher excavation costs.

How to Control Costs Without Cutting Quality

Cost control starts with the design. A simple, well-planned layout avoids the wasted steel and concrete that comes from an overcomplicated structure.

A detailed Bill of Quantities (BOQ), covering cement, steel, bricks, sand, crush, electrical conduits, plumbing pipes, labor, and shuttering, lets you compare quotations properly instead of picking a contractor on price alone, and makes hidden costs easy to spot before construction starts.

Regular site supervision matters just as much: concrete mixing ratios, steel placement, brick alignment, slab shuttering, and curing all need checking at every stage, since even good materials can't save a structure if they're used incorrectly. And once construction starts, avoid moving walls or changing the layout, since it affects steel, plumbing, electrical points, and concrete all at once, finalising the design upfront is one of the simplest ways to keep a 5 Marla grey structure on budget.

Choosing the Right Construction Company

The lowest quote isn't always the best one. A contractor who cuts the price by using weaker materials, less steel, or unskilled labor is setting you up for cracks, seepage, and repair costs down the line.

Before hiring, ask for transparent pricing, written specifications, a realistic timeline, and previous project references you can actually visit. It's also worth confirming who manages the site day to day, a proper team includes an engineer or supervisor, skilled masons, steel fixers, electricians, plumbers, and shuttering workers, each with clear responsibility.

Quality Checks We Follow During Grey Structure Construction

A strong grey structure comes from consistent checks at every stage, not one final inspection. Before excavation, we mark the site level and layout against the approved plan. During foundation work, we verify depth, reinforcement, and concrete quality.

When brick masonry begins, we check line, level, mortar ratio, and curing. During slab casting, steel reinforcement, bar spacing, slab thickness, beam placement, and conduit and plumbing sleeves are all checked before concrete is poured, and curing afterward is never skipped, since it's what gives concrete its intended strength. Waterproofing in washrooms, kitchens, and roofs is planned in from the start, not added as an afterthought.
