Building in Jubilee Town, Off Multan Road

Jubilee Town sits on the Multan Road side of Lahore, on Main Mohlanwal Road, and is commonly described as a scheme launched by the Lahore Development Authority in the late 1990s. Its residential area is divided into six blocks, A to F, and plots are mostly compact to mid-size, from around 3 Marla up to 1 Kanal. That mix shapes how we build here.

Most owners in Jubilee Town are building a family home on a plot that has to work hard. A 5 or 10 Marla plot leaves little spare land, so the plan, the structure and the site logistics all need to be settled before the first excavation. We take on grey structure and full house construction across the society, and every project starts with a free visit to your actual plot.

Check Your Plot First

Before you commit to a build, confirm your ownership papers and your plot's current approval status with the Lahore Development Authority or the relevant office. Status can differ by block and can change, and it is far easier to settle these questions before design and construction begin than after. We walk the plot with you at the site visit, but legal and approval checks belong with the authority and your lawyer.

Making Compact Plots Work

On a 3 to 5 Marla plot, the usual answer is to build up, not out. That puts more load on the foundation and the lower floors, so the structural drawings need to reflect the number of storeys you want now and any you might add later. It costs much less to design the foundation and columns for an extra floor at the start than to strengthen them afterwards.

Shared boundary walls are the other point to plan. In a block where neighbours have already built, your excavation sits close to someone else's foundation, and agreeing levels, drainage lines and wall responsibilities early avoids arguments halfway through. If you are still choosing a layout, our architectural design service prepares floor plans and structural drawings for your plot.
